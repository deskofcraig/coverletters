# Cover letter reference checklist

Use this whenever a job description (JD) is pasted. Built from the Oct 2026 review of the coverletters repo (2 interviews from ~30 applications, updated 13 Oct 2026) plus published benchmarks. Evidence from the repo is thin, so treat rules as working hypotheses and update them as results come in.

---

## 1. Intake: extract from the pasted JD

Pull these out before writing anything.

- Role type and seniority band (years asked for, "lead", "senior", contract or permanent)
- Hard requirements (must-haves, "essential", "must have")
- Nice-to-haves
- The employer's actual problem, in their own words (the sentence that describes what is broken, growing, or being built)
- Exact keywords and tool names to mirror (e.g. Storybook, Contentful, Optimizely, WCAG, React)
- Screening questions on the application form (answer these separately using STAR)
- Logistics: contract length, location or travel, remote rules, right to work, notice period, start date
- Whether a cover letter, portfolio, or sample of work is explicitly requested, and in what form

## 2. Fit triage (do this first)

Score each hard requirement: **Green** (direct evidence on record), **Amber** (adjacent evidence), **Red** (no evidence).

| Result | Action |
|---|---|
| Mostly green, no red on a hard requirement | Apply. Answer in the JD's order. |
| One or two amber on hard requirements | Apply. State the gap in one honest sentence and name the nearest evidence. |
| Any red on a hard requirement that is the core of the role (e.g. financial risk function, market research background, physical product, project management lead) | Ask Craig before drafting. Consider skipping. Recent history: these roles got no interview. |
| JD asks for 2 to 4 years or "may not have held a design job" | Either skip, or add one sentence on why this level suits Craig. |
| Same employer already applied to in the last 30 days | Check that the roles are genuinely different before applying again. |

If evidence for a requirement isn't in the profile, ask. Never stretch.

## 3. Letter blueprint

**Length:** aim for 350 to 500 words, cap about 600. Length did not separate outcomes in the repo (the two interview letters were 894 and 253 words; median across 30 letters about 580), so this rule rests on recruiter feedback (Assembly Four) and published benchmarks. A longer letter works only when every paragraph answers a JD requirement.

**Salutation and sign-off:** "For [Name] and the [Company] team," (find the hiring manager's name if findable; otherwise "For the [Company] team,"). Close with "I look forward to hearing from you," then Craig Johnson. No "Regards" or "Kind regards". The 👋 greeting only for tech and design-systems employers.

**Paragraph 1 (3 to 4 sentences)**
- Name the role.
- Name the employer's problem in their words.
- Bridge to Craig's strongest matching experience (one sentence).
- Portfolio link (deskofcraig.com) here, naming the case study that matches the problem described. No portfolio link buried at the end.

**Paragraph 2: hero story**
- One project told fully, chosen to match the JD's most important requirement.
- Weighting: Situation 20%, Task 10%, Action 60%, Result 10%. Action is what Craig personally did.
- Include the method and sample (how many interviews, test participants, users), the number, and the honest caveat only if the number looks small.

**Paragraph 3: secondary proof, in the JD's order**
- Two or three more requirements answered in one to two sentences each. Mirror the JD's keywords inside real accomplishments.
- Include one example of influence on what got built (a decision Craig changed, a scope cut, a launch delayed) when the role is senior. Needs an example from Craig.
- Include leadership proof for senior or lead roles: count of designers guided and what changed.

**Paragraph 4: gap and logistics (only what applies)**
- One honest sentence on any amber or red requirement and how Craig would close it.
- Logistics only if the JD or form asks (contract length, travel, notice, right to work).

**Close:** one sentence linking Craig to their stated mission or problem. No summary of the letter. Then the sign-off.

## 5. Rules against repeated phrasing

The recent letters share stock sentences. Recruiters at one employer (e.g. three Macquarie applications) may read several, and sources agree that formulaic, generic text is penalised.

- Each stock line may appear at most once per letter, and only when it is the hero.
- Reword it every time. Stock lines to watch: the shoe-buying analogy, "those numbers look small because it's a niche DevOps product", "AI speeds up the mechanics and understanding the problem stays with me and the team", the SSO support-ticket line, "certified Scrum Master", "10+ years".
- Rotate which proof leads. Do not default to Octopus Starter +25% in every letter.
- Use "Continuous Discovery" and "desirability, feasibility, viability, usability" at most once per letter, and only when relevant.
- Keep Craig's looser, comma-linked rhythm. Do not over-edit into uniform polish.
- Mention the AI boundary only on AI-relevant roles.

## 6. Pre-send QA checklist

- [ ] Employer name, product name and role title spelled exactly as in the JD
- [ ] Opening names the employer's problem and the role within the first two sentences
- [ ] Portfolio link in the first paragraph
- [ ] Hero story told fully, with method, sample and a number
- [ ] Every number traced to the profile. No invented figures. Pinnacle Award attributed to the billing portal only.
- [ ] Hard requirements answered in the JD's order, using its keywords
- [ ] Any gap named honestly in one sentence
- [ ] Logistics line only if asked for
- [ ] 350 to 500 words (cap ~600)
- [ ] No em dashes. No banned words or phrases from the de-AI list. No "it's not X, it's Y". No forced lists of three.
- [ ] Sentence lengths varied
- [ ] At most one everyday analogy
- [ ] Stock lines reworded and not copied from a previous letter
- [ ] Typos checked ("a la carte", "multi-disciplinary", "CoreLogic (now Cotality Australia)")
- [ ] Sign-off matches the rule
- [ ] Letter file saved in the correct repo folder with status and date in the frontmatter

## 7. Screening questions and selection criteria

Answer separately from the letter using STAR (Situation 20%, Task 10%, Action 60%, Result 10%). Keep each answer to one project. Lead with the JD's own phrasing in the situation line.

## 8. After sending

- Save the letter in the repo with the standard frontmatter: status, applied, 1st/2nd/3rd round interview, job offer, feedback. Use one date format (for example 13 Oct 2026) and check the dates run in order.
- Update the result and any feedback when it arrives. Treat "moved to unsuccessful without notice" (5 - voluntary unsuccessful) as silence, not a verdict.
- A rejection within 3 days (ProcurePro, Macquarie Risk) points to an early CV or ATS filter. Check the CV before changing the letter.
- When a result changes, review which rules above it supports or contradicts, and update this file and the cover-letter-writer skill.
