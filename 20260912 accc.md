Describe how you have applied User Experience Design skills and techniques to improve a business process or system.

I apply continuous discovery, heuristic analysis frameworks, and user & assumption testing throughout my discovery and design processes which has led to outcome-driven deliverables that have resulted in desirable and usable features and experience for products and customers.

When developing new features for Octopus Deploy, I would lead my teams in end-to-end discovery and design with outcome driven deliverables. By focusing on the outcome, I emphasise "what is the user wanting to achieve with this workflow". This started with the feature pitch for stakeholders derived from insights and gap analysis from customers and users. These insights were organised into opportunities for the teams to digest. Opportunities were discussed with stakeholders and customers for feasibility and desirability. 

By talking with customers via interviews and testing in consultancy work, user journey maps and scenarios were generated and presented. The flows and scenarios would start to lay the path for how users could reach outcomes. Through focus on key details, questions were asked to validate our assumptions when creating outcome driven experiences. This made sure the user experience being created met their expectations, allowing for their goals and needs to be achieved. When user journey maps were created, a heuristic framing was applied to make sure the experience makes sense through progressive discovery, paradigm consistency, all relevant information was available, and error correction is seamless. 

This ideation and iteration process through continuous discovery allowed for greater understanding of the problem and solution from a user's perspective and experience. 

When building interfaces, I would continue this process through wireframes, low-fidelity prototypes and interactive prototypes. These were used to validate consistency, real experience work flows, and make sure the customers use cases and scenarios were achievable. 


Describe how you have effectively worked as part of a multi-disciplinary project team to deliver high quality and timely user experience designs and outcomes.

I have spent the past 10+ years working within multi-disciplinary teams, from mobile apps to enterprise SaaS products. I have worked in this team from in-house designer positions and through consultancy where your team can change constantly. I am also a certified SCRUM Master.

When working in these teams, it is key to understand what the roles are each person is playing to help reach deliverables that are usable, desirable, feasible, and viable. 

These teams have included designers, engineers and product owners. By having this team, communication streams and relationships can be built to assist making sure all required details that lead to a successful project can happen. 

Before starting a new project, I have employed timelines and kick-offs to make sure the team is on the same page. Details of requirements, unknowns, potential risks, and time required for tasks is presented for validation and consensus via a canvas or whiteboard tool. This allows for the team to know their parts in a project from the start. These kick-off canvases are left as artefacts if required for future recall.

As work begins and continues, daily stand ups and regular syncs are employed to make sure team members and stakeholders are aware of the current shape of projects. This gives visibility to the work and can invite others into the journey of the project.

As discovery and design work is progressing, product owners, engineers, and stakeholders are organised into a RACI matrix making sure information is going to the right people. 

Handovers work best when details are presented in ways that help the next person understand what  they are required to do. Engineers are consulted in making sure interface designs and data is achievable, and if any further details are required for styling.
