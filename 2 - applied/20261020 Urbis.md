For the Urbis team,

I’m applying for the Consultant role in Future State Urban Strategy. Your posting says the work rarely starts with a simple answer, and that the job is to structure the problem, find the pattern and say what it means. That’s most of what I’ve done in design, first as a consultant at Cognizant and then in product teams at Octopus Deploy where I wrote the pitch, the requirements and the case for what went in and what waited.

At Octopus I led two joint outcomes over two years, and the first was self-service purchasing and subscription management. Customers leaving the free trial were going back and forth with sales and support to land on a plan. I ran 5 to 7 customer interviews in discovery, testing two or three assumptions in each, and they turned up needs nobody had listed at kick-off: people needed to see what changed between their old and new subscription, and the compliance checks and safety breakpoints before they agreed to buy. I added those to the user stories. Making room meant deferring the revamp of the subscription card view in Control Centre onto its own UI upgrades timeline, and that call was mine. Starter tier purchases rose 25% in the first month after I reworked the tier comparison charts with marketing, and 10 customers bought Professional without a sales conversation. With this increase desire for self-service purchasing,  sales spent less time on small accounts and announced enterprise wins more often.

At Cognizant I ran co-design workshops across health, government and education clients, wrote Statements of Work and tender documents, and presented recommendations to senior leaders. I also worked on Queensland Police service design, covering vehicle and person-of-interest recognition. So the tender, the workshop and the client presentation are familiar ground.

On the place side, I spent 16 months at CoreLogic (now Cotality Australia) working with the RP Data, ValueEx and Cordell teams on property and construction data products, and I studied civil engineering for a year before design. I haven’t worked in urban planning or economics, and I’d expect to learn the transport, economic and planning analysis from your strategists. I use Claude to cluster interview and workshop notes into themes, and I built product-helper, an open-source suite of Claude skills that checks strategy and product work at each handover. Reading what the evidence means stays with me and the team.

Being able to sit in place and understand its purpose and need to community to be insightful and joyous, peaceful and introspective. One place in Brisbane that I visit regularly is Whites Hill Reserve, a council public space and wildfire reserve less than 20 mins from the city centre. Brisbane delights in having so many larger green public space close to the city, and this is a great example. Whites Hill Reserve gives me a peaceful and joyous experience as I walk the trails through the bush, offering me time to enjoy the walk and experiences of spotting native wildlife. Generally pleasant experience every time. 

Deciding what the evidence means for a place and the people who’ll use it is the work I’d like to do with Urbis. Design thinking, fundamentals, and foundations can help strengthen a communities trust and build relationships amongst them. Through my design strengths, skills and practices, I would love to bring strategic evidence-based opportunities forward for clients, communities, and their spaces. 

I look forward to hearing from you,
Craig Johnson
