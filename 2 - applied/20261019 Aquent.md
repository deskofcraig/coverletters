Question 1
Tell us about why you would be a good fit for this position?

Answer 1
You describe a portal where medium-sized businesses manage orders, requests, billing and services, and a squad that turns a strategic UX lead's frameworks into precise, working web UI. That's close to how I've spent the last few years: self-service portals, billing experiences and the components underneath them. My portfolio is at deskofcraig.com, and the Octopus Deploy self-service purchasing case study is the nearest match.

At Octopus Deploy I led two joint outcomes over two years, and the first is the closest to this role: self-service purchasing and subscription management, covering billing, upgrades, the subscription portal and account management. Customers leaving the free trial were going back and forth with sales and technical support to land on the right plan. The business logic was fiddly. The Professional tier has five resources, each capped at 100 and bought in lots of 10, and above the cap a single click starts a sales conversation, with sales informed through Amplitude and Salesforce. I ran the customer interviews and workshops myself, then tested the selection interface in stages with marketing, sales, technical sales support and a closed user group. It ended up working like choosing size, width and colour on a pair of shoes. Starter tier purchases rose 25% in the first month and 10 customers bought Professional without a sales conversation. The base is small because it's a niche DevOps product, but sales spent less time on small accounts and started announcing enterprise wins more often.

Your posting asks for attention to edge cases, system states and component architecture. The second Octopus outcome, Policies and Compliance, produced test and evaluation results that needed interaction and feedback patterns the design system didn't have. I wrote the component request to the Foundations team, took their critique, and once it was agreed the components were worth adding, led the build with front-end engineers across Figma, Supernova and Storybook. That gave the system template selection cards with resting, hover, selected and disabled states, test status and result tags, and table rows, all on tokens, with usage guidance and state rules written in Supernova so engineers weren't guessing. I hand work over against a fixed bar: all states, all flows, annotations, component specs and WCAG AA.

At CoreLogic (now Cotality Australia) I owned UX for the customer billing portal, which won a 2021 Pinnacle Award, and designed a single sign-on experience across 4 products. Before design I spent 11 years as an insurance broker handling amendments, renewals, cancellations and payment reconciling for business clients, so orders, changes and billing are familiar from the other side of the counter. I haven't worked in telecommunications, so I'd lean on the squad for the product catalogue and its rules.

A business customer who can place an order or fix a bill without ringing anyone is the outcome I'd want to help this squad deliver.

I look forward to hearing from you,
Craig Johnson

Question 2
Your resume highlights extensive experience designing enterprise SaaS and B2B platforms. Could you share a specific example where your design for a complex B2B portal significantly improved user efficiency or achieved key business outcomes?

Answer 2
At Octopus Deploy, technical operations teams were losing days to manual back and forth whenever a deployment failed, because nobody could find the cause until it broke at runtime. I led discovery, design and delivery of the Policies and Compliance workflows with product leads and engineers. That covered creating and viewing policies, templates and starter code, and testing and evaluating a policy before it runs. I ran rolling customer interviews and wrote the early requirements, then designed pre-runtime compliance testing so a deployment that would break is caught before it runs. Operations engineers had to trust that a guardrail would stop a bad deployment, so I designed the test status and result tags to be readable at a glance. The design system had no components for that, so I wrote the request to the Foundations team and led the build with front-end engineers across Figma, Supernova and Storybook. A failed deployment now takes hours to correct instead of days. I don’t have a hard metric beyond that.

Question 3
I'm impressed by the 25% increase in Starter tier purchases you achieved at Octopus Deploy. Could you briefly describe the key design strategies you implemented for the self-service purchasing experience that led to this significant business outcome?

Answer 3 
Three decisions did most of the work. Customers leaving the free trial were going back and forth with sales and technical support to find the right plan, so the first job was making the three tiers easy to compare. I worked with marketing to redevelop the pricing and tier comparison guides, and the 25% rise in Starter purchases in the first month came from that rework. Second, I redesigned the upgrade flow from free trial to paid so it changes with the customer’s current subscription, with invitations and calls to action that show what locked enterprise features could do.

Third, I designed the Professional tier selection. It has five resources, each capped at 100 and bought in lots of 10, and above the cap one click starts a sales conversation, with sales informed through Amplitude and Salesforce. I tested it in stages with marketing, sales, stakeholders and a closed user group, and we landed on something like choosing size, width and colour for a pair of shoes. Ten customers bought Professional on their own in the first month. The numbers are small because it’s a niche DevOps product, but sales spent less time on small accounts and started announcing enterprise wins more often.
