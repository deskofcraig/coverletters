```
Trilogy Care | Digital | Brisbane, QLD

At Trilogy Care, our mission is to empower older Australians to live independently in the home they love for longer.

 

We’re looking for a Product Manager to shape the evolution of our connected digital platform, including the Core Portal used by internal teams, the consumer app for clients and families, as well as for supplier-facing systems.

 

Working closely with our product team for designing, engineering and collaborating with stakeholders, you’ll own initiatives from discovery and scoping through to delivery, launch and measurable outcomes.

 

Trilogy Care is an AI-forward organisation. You’ll help identify opportunities for AI, automation and emerging technologies while ensuring privacy, security and responsible data use are built into every product decision.

 

If you combine customer empathy, commercial judgement, systems thinking and technical understanding, we’d love to hear from you.

 

What you’ll be doing

 

Product ownership and delivery

Own initiatives end to end, from discovery and prioritisation through development, release and post-launch evaluation.
Define problem statements, requirements, user stories and acceptance criteria.
Maintain a prioritised roadmap balancing customer needs, business value, technical feasibility, risk and delivery capacity.

Discovery and solution design

Stakeholder collaboration

AI, privacy and innovation

Identify opportunities to improve products and workflows through AI, automation and emerging technologies.

Embed privacy, security, consent and data minimisation into product design, protecting sensitive personal, health and financial information.

End-to-end product management: Experience leading initiatives from discovery to delivery and measuring outcomes.
```

# Cover letter / Application Questions:
```
For the Trilogy Care team,

I’m applying for the Product Manager role on your connected platform: the Core Portal, the consumer app and the supplier systems. I’ve worked as a designer for 10 years, and the part of my work that sits closest to product management is finding the problem, writing it up as requirements and user stories, deciding what ships first, then checking whether a number moved.

At Octopus, customers leaving a free trial were going back and forth with sales and technical support to land on the right plan. I ran 5 to 7 customer interviews in discovery, testing 2 or 3 assumptions in each, and pulled in marketing, sales and support when customers were hard to reach. After kick-off we found the upgrade flow needed more than we’d scoped. Customers had to understand what changed between their old and new subscription, and they needed compliance checks and safety breakpoints before agreeing to buy. I wrote those into the user stories, and I made the call to move the subscription card view revamp to a separate Control Centre timeline to make room. After I reworked the tier comparison charts with marketing, Starter purchases rose 25% in the first month and 10 customers bought Professional without a sales conversation. The base is small for a niche enterprise product, but sales spent less time on small accounts and announced enterprise wins more often.

Billing and sensitive information are familiar ground. I owned UX for the CoreLogic billing portal (2021 Pinnacle Award) and built payment flows at Amber Labs and Octopus. At Cognizant I led the design system for Healius pathology, covering technicians, couriers, lab staff and patients, so I’ve handled health results across several user groups. At Amber Labs I wrote acceptance criteria for developers, signed off QA before release, and moved planning from 2-week to 3-month horizons while output rose. I’ve built React components with front-end engineers and worked on portals that pass data between CMS backends and APIs. For older users, I spent a summer at UQ’s Design Research Lab testing tactile interfaces with people living with dementia, and I hold work to WCAG AA.

On AI, I built an agentic AI product team at Octopus (design, engineer and product owner roles) to speed up analysis and decisions, and I wrote product-helper, an open-source set of Claude skills that checks work at each handover. AI handles the mechanics, and understanding the problem stays with me and the team.

I haven’t held a Product Manager title, and I haven’t worked in aged care or shipped an AI feature to customers. I’d expect to learn the funding rules and privacy obligations from your Care Operations and Finance teams.

A coordinator, a client’s family and a supplier each getting what they need from one platform is what keeps older Australians in the homes they love, and I’d like to help build it.

I look forward to hearing from you,
Craig Johnson
```
